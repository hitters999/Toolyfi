❓ FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about EMI and loan calculations.

What is EMI? +
EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is the fixed amount you pay to a lender every month to repay a loan. Each EMI payment consists of two components: the principal repayment and the interest charge. Over time, the interest portion decreases and the principal portion increases.
How is EMI calculated? +
EMI is calculated using the standard amortization formula: EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1), where P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of monthly installments.
How can I reduce my EMI? +
You can reduce your EMI in three ways: (1) Reduce the loan amount by making a larger down payment. (2) Negotiate a lower interest rate with your lender. (3) Extend the loan tenure — a longer repayment period means lower monthly EMI, though you'll pay more total interest.
What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance rate? +
A flat interest rate is calculated on the original loan amount throughout the loan tenure. A reducing balance rate is calculated on the outstanding principal, which decreases with each EMI payment. Our calculator uses the reducing balance method.
Is it better to have a shorter or longer loan tenure? +
A shorter tenure means higher EMI but less total interest paid. A longer tenure means lower EMI but significantly more total interest. Use the sliders in this tool to compare both scenarios.
Does prepayment reduce EMI or loan tenure? +
Prepayment can either reduce your EMI or shorten your loan tenure, depending on your lender's policy. Most financial advisors recommend choosing to reduce the tenure, as this saves more interest over the life of the loan.

Loan EMI Calculator — The Complete 2026 Guide

Taking a loan is one of the most significant financial decisions you can make. Whether it's a home mortgage, a car loan, or a personal loan, understanding exactly how much you will pay each month — and how much of that is interest — is essential before signing any loan agreement.

This complete guide explains EMI, how it is calculated, what affects your monthly payment, and how to use this free EMI calculator to plan your loan smarter. All calculations are done instantly in your browser with no signup required.

✅ Quick Answer: EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. Enter your loan amount, interest rate, and tenure above to calculate your exact EMI instantly.

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is the fixed monthly amount a borrower pays to repay a loan over a specified period. Each EMI payment consists of two components: the principal portion (repayment of the original loan amount) and the interest portion (the cost of borrowing).

In the early months of a loan, a larger portion of the EMI goes toward interest and a smaller portion reduces the principal. Over time, this ratio reverses — more of each payment goes toward reducing the principal. This is called loan amortization.

How is EMI Calculated?

The standard EMI formula used by banks and financial institutions worldwide is:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n
          ─────────────────────
             (1 + r)^n − 1

Where:
P = Principal loan amount
r = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
n = Number of monthly installments (loan tenure in months)

Example Calculation

For a $100,000 loan at 8.5% annual interest for 5 years (60 months):

  • P = $100,000
  • r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00708
  • n = 60
  • EMI = $2,028 per month

Total repayment = $2,028 × 60 = $121,665. Total interest paid = $21,665 — which is 17.8% of the loan amount.

Factors That Affect Your EMI

1. Loan Amount (Principal)

The higher the loan amount, the higher the EMI. You can reduce your loan amount by making a larger down payment.

2. Interest Rate

Even a 1% difference in interest rate can change your EMI substantially on large loans. Always compare rates from multiple lenders before finalizing your loan.

3. Loan Tenure

Longer tenure = lower EMI, but higher total interest paid. Shorter tenure = higher EMI, but lower total interest.

How to Reduce Your EMI or Total Interest

  • Make a larger down payment — Reduces the principal, directly reducing EMI
  • Improve your credit score — Higher credit scores unlock lower interest rates
  • Negotiate with lenders — Banks often negotiate rates for good customers
  • Make prepayments — Extra payments toward principal reduce future interest significantly
  • Compare multiple lenders — Even 0.5% rate difference matters significantly on large loans

EMI Planning Best Practices

  • Keep EMI below 40% of income — Financial advisors recommend your total EMI payments not exceed 40% of your monthly take-home income
  • Build an emergency fund first — Have 3–6 months of EMI payments saved before taking a loan
  • Calculate total cost, not just EMI — Always look at total repayment, not just the monthly number
