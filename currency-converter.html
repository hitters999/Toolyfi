How Exchange Rates Work

An exchange rate tells you how much of one currency you get for one unit of another. For example, if the USD/EUR rate is 0.92, it means 1 US Dollar is worth 0.92 Euros.

Exchange rates fluctuate constantly based on:

  • Supply and demand — High demand for a currency pushes its value up
  • Interest rates — Countries with higher interest rates attract more foreign investment, strengthening their currency
  • Inflation — Lower inflation generally means a stronger currency
  • Political stability — Stable governments attract investment; instability weakens currency
  • Trade balance — Countries that export more than they import tend to have stronger currencies
  • Market sentiment — Speculation and investor confidence play a significant role

💡 The mid-market rate (also called interbank rate) is the true exchange rate — the midpoint between buy and sell rates. Our tool shows mid-market rates, which are the most accurate reference point.

Major Currency Pairs Explained

USD — US Dollar

The US Dollar is the world's primary reserve currency, used in approximately 88% of all forex transactions. It is the benchmark against which most other currencies are measured. When people say "the dollar is strong," they mean the USD buys more of other currencies.

EUR — Euro

The Euro is the second most traded currency in the world and the official currency of 20 European Union member states. The EUR/USD pair is the most traded forex pair globally, accounting for about 23% of daily forex volume.

GBP — British Pound Sterling

The British Pound is one of the oldest and most valuable currencies in the world. It is sometimes called "Cable" in forex circles, a term dating back to when exchange rates were transmitted between London and New York via transatlantic cable.

PKR — Pakistani Rupee

The Pakistani Rupee is widely searched for by the Pakistani diaspora sending remittances home, as well as businesses engaged in trade with Pakistan. The USD/PKR rate is one of the most commonly searched currency conversions globally among South Asian users.

AED — UAE Dirham

The UAE Dirham has been pegged to the US Dollar since 1997 at a fixed rate of approximately 3.6725 AED per USD. This peg makes it one of the most stable currencies for Gulf-region calculations.

Mid-Market Rate vs Bank Rate

There is an important distinction between the mid-market rate shown in our tool and the rate you actually receive from a bank or money transfer service.

The mid-market rate is the fair, unbiased midpoint between the buy and sell rates. It is what you see on financial news sites and what our tool displays.

The bank rate includes a spread — the bank's profit margin — added on top of the mid-market rate. Banks typically add 1 to 5% for retail customers. Airport exchange desks may add 7 to 15% or more.

This is why the amount you receive when exchanging currency at a bank or airport is always less than what our converter shows. Our tool gives you the true reference rate to compare against what any service is offering you.

How to Get the Best Exchange Rate

  • Avoid airport kiosks — They offer the worst rates, often 10-15% worse than mid-market.
  • Use online money transfer services — Wise (formerly TransferWise), Remitly, and similar services offer rates much closer to mid-market than banks.
  • Use a multi-currency card — Cards like Wise Debit or Revolut convert at or near mid-market rates with minimal fees.
  • Draw cash from ATMs abroad — Usually better than exchange kiosks, but check your bank's foreign transaction fees first.
  • Never exchange at hotels — Hotel exchange rates are almost always worse than banks or ATMs.
  • Check rates before you travel — Know the mid-market rate so you can identify whether you are being offered a fair deal.

Currency Conversion for Remittances

Remittances — money sent by workers abroad to their home countries — represent one of the largest financial flows in the world. In 2023, global remittances to low and middle income countries exceeded $650 billion, according to World Bank data.

For millions of Pakistani, Indian, Filipino, and African workers abroad, getting the best exchange rate directly impacts how much their families receive. A 1% difference in rate on a $1,000 transfer means $10 lost — which is significant in many economies.

Our currency converter helps you compare the mid-market rate against what remittance services are offering, so you can choose the best option for your transfers.
