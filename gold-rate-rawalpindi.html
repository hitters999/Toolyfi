Gold Rate in Rawalpindi Today — Complete Guide 2026

Rawalpindi — "Pindi" to millions — is one of Pakistan's most important gold markets, home to the legendary Raja Bazaar Sarafa, one of the largest and most competitive wholesale gold markets in the entire country. The gold rate in Rawalpindi today follows the national benchmark set by Karachi's Sarafa Jewellers Association each morning, with the Rawalpindi Sarafa Association announcing the local rate by 10:30 AM. Unlike Islamabad's organized sector-market retail environment, Rawalpindi's gold market is rooted in its ancient bazaar culture — organic, competitive, and built for volume.

As of today, the 24 karat gold rate in Rawalpindi per tola is PKR 503,270. For 22 karat gold — the standard for bridal and everyday jewelry — the rate stands at PKR 461,330 per tola. What makes Rawalpindi unique is not the base rate (which is the same across Pakistan) but the significantly lower making charges available at Raja Bazaar compared to any organized retail market in the country.

Rawalpindi's gold market is critically important for several buyer segments: military families (GHQ and Chaklala Garrison are based here), wholesale dealers sourcing for retailers in KPK and AJK, bridal families from Islamabad who cross the twin-city boundary for better prices, and overseas Pakistanis returning from Gulf countries who make large gold purchases during home visits.

📍 Key Fact — Raja Bazaar's Wholesale Power

Raja Bazaar Sarafa is a wholesale market. Gold dealers travel from Abbottabad, Mirpur AJK, Attock, Jhelum, and Chakwal to source gold here. If you are buying 3 tolas or more, Raja Bazaar making charges — typically PKR 700–1,200 per gram — can save PKR 10,000–30,000 compared to Islamabad's organized retail shops.

Raja Bazaar Sarafa — Pakistan's Wholesale Gold Powerhouse

Raja Bazaar is the commercial heart of old Rawalpindi — a dense, labyrinthine market stretching across several city blocks near Raja Bazaar Chowk. Its gold section, known as Sarafa Bazaar, has been operating for over a century and represents the most competitive gold pricing environment in northern Pakistan. Hundreds of dealers operate from shops, open-air stalls, and dedicated gold exchange centres, creating a level of price competition that systematically pushes making charges lower than anywhere in Islamabad or even Lahore's comparable markets.

The Rawalpindi Sarafa Association represents registered gold dealers and announces the daily gold rate each morning after the Karachi benchmark is published. Association members include the largest wholesale dealers who supply jewelry shops across the greater Punjab-KPK region. The market's wholesale character means buyers with serious purchasing intent — large bridal sets, investment gold, institutional buyers — routinely find the best deals in Pakistan here.

For retail buyers visiting Raja Bazaar for the first time, the environment can seem overwhelming compared to Islamabad's organized sector markets. Dozens of dealers may call out to potential customers, prices for making charges can vary widely, and the traditional negotiation culture requires confidence. However, for buyers who do their research (starting with checking today's rate on this page), Raja Bazaar consistently delivers the best value for gold jewelry purchases in the twin cities region.

Saddar Bazar — Where Military Meets Market

Saddar Bazar in Rawalpindi's cantonment area is the city's most organized retail gold market, catering primarily to the military community stationed at GHQ, Chaklala Garrison, and the various military installations in Rawalpindi Cantonment. Gold shops in Saddar tend to be more formal than Raja Bazaar — fixed price displays are more common, receipts are standard, and the dealer-customer dynamic is more professional. Making charges in Saddar are slightly higher than Raja Bazaar but lower than Islamabad, making it the preferred shopping destination for army officers' families who want competitive prices with a more organized retail experience.

Committee Chowk and Murree Road

Committee Chowk and the Murree Road corridor represent Rawalpindi's transition from the old bazaar economy to modern retail. Gold shops here offer a mix of traditional Pakistani jewelry and more modern designs, with making charges that fall between the ultra-competitive Raja Bazaar rates and the organized premium of Islamabad. For buyers who live in Rawalpindi's newer residential areas — Satellite Town, Gulraiz, PWD Colony — these markets offer a convenient middle ground without the density and negotiation intensity of Raja Bazaar.

How Rawalpindi's Gold Rate is Determined

Like all Pakistani cities, Rawalpindi gold prices are ultimately driven by two international factors: the London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) gold price in USD per troy ounce, and the State Bank of Pakistan's USD/PKR exchange rate. Each morning, these two numbers are combined and adjusted for Pakistan's import duty and taxes to produce the base Karachi Sarafa rate — which then becomes the national benchmark.

The Rawalpindi Sarafa Association receives this benchmark and announces it to local dealers. Minor adjustments may be made for local market conditions, but in practice the Rawalpindi rate varies from the Karachi rate by no more than PKR 100–200 per tola. Rawalpindi vs. Islamabad Gold Rate — The Twin Cities Comparison

Rawalpindi and Islamabad together form Pakistan's "twin cities" — a single metropolitan area where the administrative capital and the historic garrison city have grown together. Despite being 20 minutes apart, their gold markets are strikingly different in character, pricing, and buyer experience.

On the base gold rate, both cities are identical — they follow the same Karachi Sarafa benchmark. Every difference comes from making charges and dealer premiums. Rawalpindi's Raja Bazaar, with its hundreds of competing wholesale dealers, drives making charges systematically lower than Islamabad's organized sector markets. A 22K bangle that costs PKR 2,000 per gram in making charges at Jinnah Super Islamabad can typically be found for PKR 1,500 or less at Raja Bazaar — a difference of PKR 500 per gram that adds up significantly on large purchases.

For a typical bridal gold set of 4–5 tolas (approximately 47–58 grams), the making charge saving at Raja Bazaar versus Islamabad ranges from PKR 20,000 to PKR 35,000. This is why families from Islamabad's most affluent sectors regularly drive to Raja Bazaar for bridal gold purchases, even when they could easily afford Islamabad's premium markets.

Islamabad's advantage lies in post-purchase support: better documentation, more reliable exchange and refund policies, and a more regulated environment. For certified hallmarked jewelry or diamond-set pieces, Islamabad's mall-based stores are the better choice. For pure gold value — maximum karat weight for minimum total price — Raja Bazaar remains unbeatable.

Buying Gold in Rawalpindi — Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1 — Check Today's Rate on This Page

Before stepping into any shop in Raja Bazaar or Saddar, check the current 22K or 24K gold rate per gram. This is your baseline. Any dealer's quoted "gold value" portion of the bill should match this rate exactly — if it is higher, negotiate or walk away.

Step 2 — Understand the Two-Part Price

Gold jewelry price = Gold value (weight × rate per gram × karat purity) + Making charges (jadai per gram). In Raja Bazaar, making charges for simple pieces like bangles and rings range from PKR 700 to PKR 1,500 per gram. For intricate bridal pieces, they may reach PKR 2,000–2,500. Always ask for both numbers separately. A dealer who refuses to separate them is a red flag.

Step 3 — Get a Weight Certificate

For purchases above 2 tolas, insist on a weight certificate from an independent scale, or watch the weighing yourself. Raja Bazaar has government-certified weighing services available. The purity stamp (916 for 22K, 999 for 24K) should be clearly visible on the piece.

Step 4 — Negotiate Making Charges

In Raja Bazaar, making charges are negotiable — unlike fixed-price mall stores. For bulk purchases (3+ tolas), dealers will typically reduce making charges by PKR 100–300 per gram. Cash payment may yield an additional small discount. The gold rate itself is not negotiable — it is set by the Sarafa Association.

Gold Investment in Rawalpindi

Rawalpindi's military establishment and large middle class make it one of Pakistan's most active gold investment markets. Common investment approaches include physical gold bars and coins available from established Raja Bazaar dealers, 24K investment-grade gold available in 1-gram to 100-gram sizes, and PMEX gold futures accessible online from any brokerage account. Investment gold buyers in Rawalpindi have the advantage of access to wholesale prices at Raja Bazaar — the tightest buy-sell spreads in northern Pakistan.

Seasonal Gold Demand in Rawalpindi

Rawalpindi's gold demand follows a distinctive seasonal pattern shaped by its military community and traditional culture. Peak demand periods include the main wedding season (October–December), pre-Eid (Eid-ul-Fitr gold gifting), and notably the summer military promotion cycle when officers' families often mark promotions with gold purchases — a Rawalpindi-specific pattern rarely seen in civilian cities. AJK and KPK buyers also increase their Raja Bazaar visits during summer school holidays when inter-provincial travel increases.

Frequently Asked Questions — Gold Rate Rawalpindi

Q: Is Raja Bazaar Sarafa safe for gold shopping?
A: Yes. Raja Bazaar Sarafa has been operating for over a century and has an established dealer community with strong reputational accountability. The market is busy and well-known, making fraudulent activity relatively rare. For large purchases, use the Rawalpindi Sarafa Association's registered dealer list or ask for recommendations from trusted locals.

Q: Can I get my gold tested in Raja Bazaar?
A: Yes. Electronic gold testers are available at most established shops in Raja Bazaar. Acid testing is also standard. For major purchases, request an independent test — several shops in the bazaar provide this service at minimal cost.

Q: What is the difference between Pindi gold rate and Lahore gold rate?
A: The base gold rate is identical in both cities. Lahore's Anarkali Sarafa and Rawalpindi's Raja Bazaar are both highly competitive wholesale markets with similarly low making charges — though making charge levels and jewelry styles differ. For buyers in upper Punjab and KPK, Raja Bazaar is more accessible.

Q: Do shops in Raja Bazaar give receipts?
A: Established shops give receipts, but not all open-air stalls do. Always insist on a proper receipt showing item weight, karat, gold rate per gram, gold value total, making charges per gram, and grand total — especially for purchases above PKR 50,000.
