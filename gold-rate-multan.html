Gold Rate in Multan Today — The Complete Market Guide 2026

Welcome to the most comprehensive guide to tracking the gold rate in Multan today. Known as the "City of Saints" and standing as the economic and cultural capital of South Punjab, Multan boasts a gold market that is deeply entrenched in centuries of tradition, agricultural wealth, and unique local craftsmanship. Whether you are a Zamindar arriving from Lodhran post-harvest to purchase heavy traditional sets, or a modern family shopping in the air-conditioned boutiques of Gulgasht Colony, understanding Multan's specific Sarafa dynamics is crucial.

As of today, the 24 karat gold rate in Multan per tola is PKR 503,270. For the standard 22 karat gold—which is universally used for bridal jewelry across South Punjab—the price stands at PKR 461,330 per tola. While this base rate is synced with the national Karachi benchmark, the true cost of purchasing gold in Multan relies heavily on where you shop and what type of craftsmanship you demand.

Multan’s gold economy is fascinating because it is entirely distinctive from northern cities like Islamabad or Lahore. It is driven by the vast agricultural heartland of the Seraiki belt. When the cotton, wheat, and world-famous mango harvests are completed, millions of rupees flow into the city, directly triggering massive gold buying sprees in historic markets like Hussain Agahi.

📍 Key Fact — The Hussain Agahi Advantage

Hussain Agahi Bazaar is the wholesale center of South Punjab's gold trade. Because the volume of buying is so immense, particularly from rural buyers, dealers operate on high turnover rather than high margins. If you are buying heavy 22K bridal sets, the making charges (jadai) here are generally PKR 400 to PKR 800 per gram cheaper than in Gulgasht Colony or Bosan Road malls.

Multan’s Primary Gold Markets: A Deep Dive

Multan presents a stark contrast between its ancient, bustling bazaars and its rapidly developing modern commercial sectors. Knowing which market suits your needs will save you significant money and time.

1. Hussain Agahi & Sarafa Bazaar — The Historic Core

Located in the heart of the old city near the Ghanta Ghar (Clock Tower), Hussain Agahi is one of the oldest and most legendary commercial centers in Pakistan. Its dedicated Sarafa Bazaar is a dense network of traditional jewelers who have served South Punjabi families for generations.

Inside this area lies Andhi Khui (The Blind Well), which functions as the manufacturing and bullion trading hub. Here, raw gold is melted, refined, and crafted.
Best For: Heavy traditional 22K bridal sets, pure 24K gold pathis (biscuits) for investment, and buyers seeking the absolute lowest making charges in Multan through firm negotiation.

2. Gulgasht Colony — The Modern Premium Sector

As Multan's urban middle and upper classes expanded, the demand for a more comfortable, luxurious shopping experience grew. Gulgasht Colony, particularly its main commercial avenues, answered this call. The jewelry boutiques here feature elegant lighting, air conditioning, and a calm environment—a sharp contrast to the chaotic energy of Hussain Agahi.

Best For: Elite families, corporate professionals, and modern brides. While the base gold rate is the same, these boutiques charge a premium for their "brand value," ambience, and modern imported designs (like Turkish or Dubai styles). Expect making charges to be noticeably higher.

3. Multan Cantonment (Saddar)

The Cantonment area offers highly organized retail spaces with strict security protocols. Jewelers in Saddar cater primarily to military families stationed in Multan and local affluent residents who prioritize safety and transparency over aggressive bargaining.

Best For: Mid-to-heavy 22K jewelry with clear, often fixed-price structures. Customer service and post-purchase support (like resizing and polishing) are excellent here.

The Art of Multani Gold: Meenakari and Tradition

You cannot discuss the gold rate per gram in Multan today without mentioning the city's unique contribution to Pakistani jewelry: Multani Meenakari.

Meenakari is the ancient art of coloring and ornamenting the surface of metals by fusing brilliant colors. Multani craftsmen are nationally renowned for their intricate enamel work, predominantly using deep blues, rich greens, and vibrant reds set against pure 22K yellow gold. Heavy bridal sets—often featuring large Jhumkas, Gulubands (chokers), and broad bangles—are intricately painted with this enamel.

When calculating your total cost in Multan, remember that heavy Meenakari work requires exceptional labor. Therefore, the making charges on authentic Multani enameled sets will be slightly higher than plain gold sets, but the cultural and aesthetic value is unmatched in Pakistan.

The Economics of Gold in South Punjab

To truly grasp the dynamics of Multan's gold market, one must look at the surrounding geography. Multan is the nucleus of Pakistan’s most fertile agricultural belt, bordering Shujabad, Lodhran, Vehari, Khanewal, and Muzaffargarh.

The Agricultural Influx (Mangoes & Cotton)

In South Punjab, traditional wealth is measured in land and gold. Following the major harvest seasons—particularly the summer mango season and the autumn cotton season—Zamindars (landowners) receive massive cash payouts. To protect this wealth from inflation, it is immediately converted into gold. This creates massive, highly predictable seasonal rushes in Hussain Agahi. During these harvest months, jewelers are incredibly busy, and securing discounts on making charges becomes much more difficult.

💡 Multan Buyer's Tip: Timing Your Purchase

If you are planning a wedding and are budget-conscious, try to purchase your gold during the "off-season" (late winter or early spring). Avoid buying immediately after the mango or cotton harvests when rural liquidity is highest, as local jewelers will be less willing to negotiate on jadai (making charges).

How the Multan Gold Rate is Calculated

The daily rate you see on this page is derived through a precise economic formula, not arbitrary local decisions:

  1. International Bullion (LBMA): The starting point is the global price of gold in US Dollars per ounce.
  2. USD to PKR Parity: Because gold is an imported commodity, the strength of the Rupee dictates the local price. If the Rupee depreciates, the gold rate in Multan instantly rises.
  3. Karachi Benchmark: The Karachi Sarafa Association calculates the base national rate after factoring in import duties and taxes.
  4. Multan Sarafa Association: Local leaders in Hussain Agahi receive the Karachi rate every morning and announce it to the city. Multan's rate perfectly mirrors the national rate, with perhaps a negligible PKR 50–100 variance for bullion transportation.

Multan vs. Lahore Gold Market Comparison

Historically, wealthy South Punjab families would travel to Lahore (Shah Alam or Liberty) for major bridal purchases. Today, that is largely unnecessary. Here is why:

  • Identical Base Rate: The 24K and 22K base rates are identical in both cities.
  • Better Making Charges: Hussain Agahi consistently offers lower making charges than Lahore’s premium markets.
  • Cultural Designs: If you want authentic Seraiki/Multani traditional designs (heavy sets, Meenakari), local Multan artisans execute this far better than Lahore jewelers who favor lighter, cosmopolitan styles.
  • The Verdict: Unless you are specifically seeking certified 18K diamond-polki fusion jewelry (where Lahore excels), Multan offers superior value and better traditional craftsmanship for 22K bridal gold.

Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Gold in Multan

Step 1: Verify the Live Rate

Before stepping into Hussain Agahi or Gulgasht, use the Toolyfi calculator on this page. Input your desired weight to see the exact base "Pure Gold Value."

Step 2: Isolate the Jadai (Making Charges)

When quoted a final price for a set, ask the jeweler to mathematically separate the pure gold value from the making charges. The gold value is non-negotiable. The jadai is entirely negotiable.

Step 3: Negotiate Based on Volume

In Multan, if you are buying a 5-tola set, the jeweler has a large profit margin. If they quote PKR 1,800 per gram for making, counter with PKR 1,000. Be polite but firm. Traditional Sarafa jewelers respect buyers who know the math.

Step 4: Demand Computerized Testing

Modern jewelers in Multan use XRF (X-ray fluorescence) machines. Ensure your 22K jewelry is tested in front of you. The machine should read 91.6% purity.

Step 5: Secure a Detailed Invoice

A legally binding receipt in Multan should include: The date, exact grammage, karat purity (22K), applied daily gold rate, total making charges, and the shop’s specific deduction policy if you ever return the jewelry for cash or exchange.

Investment Gold in Multan

For investors and Zamindars looking to preserve wealth without paying making charges, 24K pure gold is the standard. In Multan, this is typically purchased in the form of local Pathis (smelted bars) from trusted bullion dealers in Andhi Khui, or as sealed, imported Pamp Suisse/ARY bars which carry a slight premium but guarantee international certification.
