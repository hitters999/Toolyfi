Common use cases

Travel & weather: If you're checking a forecast from a US site while living in a Celsius country (or vice versa), converting the day's high and low takes the guesswork out of packing.

Health tracking: Thermometers sold in different countries use different scales. Converting a child's or your own reading against the fever threshold above helps you react appropriately without confusion.

Science & engineering: Kelvin is the SI base unit for thermodynamic calculations. Students and engineers often need to shift between lab notation (K) and everyday reporting (°C).

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit? ▾
Multiply by 9/5 (1.8), then add 32. Example: 20°C × 1.8 = 36, + 32 = 68°F.
How do I convert Fahrenheit to Celsius? ▾
Subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9. Example: 98.6°F − 32 = 66.6, × 5/9 = 37°C.
What is 0 Kelvin in Celsius? ▾
0 K equals −273.15°C, known as absolute zero — the coldest temperature theoretically possible.
Is there a quick mental shortcut? ▾
Double the Celsius value and add 30 for a rough Fahrenheit estimate, accurate within about 5–8°F for everyday temperatures.
Why does Kelvin never go negative? ▾
Kelvin starts at absolute zero, the lowest possible energy state, so there is no value below 0 K in standard physics.
What temperature counts as a fever? ▾
38°C (100.4°F) or higher is generally considered a fever, versus a normal average of 37°C (98.6°F).
